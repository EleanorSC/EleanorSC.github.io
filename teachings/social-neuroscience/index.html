Social Neuroscience Tutorials

A tutorial-based course exploring how neural systems give rise to social cognition, language, memory, personality, morality, attachment, and interpersonal understanding.

Instructor: Dr Eleanor Conole

Term: Michaelmas

Location: Lady Margaret Hall, University of Oxford

Time: Thursdays, 11:00 AM / 2:00 PM

Course Overview

This tutorial course examines how neural systems generate social cognition, communication, emotion, and interpersonal behaviour.

Drawing on cognitive neuroscience, neuropsychology, psychiatry, and social psychology, students will critically evaluate the biological basis of:

  • Memory and autobiographical identity
  • Language and social communication
  • Personality and individual differences
  • Motivation and reward
  • Morality and moral emotion
  • Attachment and bonding
  • Metacognition and social self-awareness

The course integrates evidence from:

  • Lesion studies
  • Neuroimaging
  • Neuropsychology
  • Developmental neuroscience
  • Clinical disorders

Teaching Format

Tutorials are discussion-based and focus on:

  • Essay construction
  • Critical evaluation of evidence
  • Mechanistic explanation
  • Linking brain systems to social behaviour
  • Oxford-style tutorial discussion

Students are expected to prepare a 1,000–1,500 word essay each week.

Core Themes

  • Social cognition
  • Emotion and affective neuroscience
  • Neural representation of self and others
  • Language and shared meaning
  • Moral judgement and behaviour
  • Reward, motivation, and addiction
  • Personality structure and psychopathology
  • Attachment and developmental social neuroscience

Recommended Textbooks

  • Banich & Compton — Cognitive Neuroscience
  • Ward — The Student’s Guide to Cognitive Neuroscience
  • Frith & Frith — Social Cognition in Humans
  • Adolphs — The Social Brain
  • Gazzaniga, Ivry & Mangun — Cognitive Neuroscience
